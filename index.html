Real-time Fluid Migration Detection

2026 · SickKids (PCIGITI) · Computer Vision, PyTorch, U-Net, ResNet-34, OpenCV

Problem

Watch a live, low-contrast grayscale video stream and detect the moment an injected dark fluid spreads into a user-defined region of the image. Almost no labeled data, extreme class imbalance, and a tight latency and precision budget.

What I tried

  • Straight pixel thresholding on a 3D-printed flow simulator. It worked as a proof of concept.
  • On Simone(Pulsatile Flow Model Mimicing Heart's Pump) under X-ray, the motors and metallic hardware swamped the frame, so the detector had to explicitly reject metal-like pixels, and I moved to a third simulator with no metal.
  • A five-stage false-positive cascade, each stage aimed at a specific, observed failure mode. It worked, but it was hand-tuned, and every threshold would need re-tuning for a new setup.
  • Replaced it with a U-Net with a ResNet-34 encoder, ImageNet-pretrained, trained on 19 hand-annotated frames with BCE + Dice loss.
  • Automatic region proposal: YOLO performed poorly at Dice 0.41. A U-Net/ResNet-34 fine-tuned on retinal vessel data roughly doubled that to Dice 0.82.

Result

  • Latency went from roughly 350ms to roughly 45ms, running 1–2 frames behind the live feed.
  • Trained entirely on simulated frames, Dice fell only from 0.85 to 0.80 on real-world imaging, and recall held at 0.88.
  • Precision dropped from 0.82 to 0.73, which is the honest limitation.
